Numbers 15:37–41

39It shall be to you for a fringe, that you may see it, and remember all Yahweh’s commandments, and do them; and that you don’t follow your own heart and your own eyes, after which you used to play the prostitute;

40so that you may remember and do all my commandments, and be holy to your God.

41I am Yahweh your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am Yahweh your God.”

World English Bible (WEB) · Public Domain

This command was given to Israel: a blue cord on the tassels of their garments, visible as a call to remember and obey God’s commandments. One Blue Thread borrows that image; it does not replace the practice.

the practice

Scripture is the voice.

One Blue Thread helps you arrive, read, remember, and return. It does not summarise the passage or manufacture a devotional around it. Once the text is before you, the app becomes quiet.

zone 05 · the weave

Your book, woven.

One thread per chapter, strung before you start. Each day you read passes the weft through once. Gaps stay gaps — a mirror, not a threat.

the lab · beneath the weave

how it learns

Dormant for a year. Then it learns quietly.

An app with a million readers optimises for the average one. One Blue Thread has exactly one. For your first year it sends the same plain reminder, always. Only after that does it start choosing which kind of nudge actually gets you back to the page.

for example — the reminder it's choosing between

Day 1–365dormant, one plain reminder Day 366 onechoes your cue, stays neutral, or says nothing — whichever gets you back

It only ever changes whether and when it nudges — never which day you're meant to read, how much, or the seal itself.

One tap freezes it, permanently, whenever you like — it advises, it doesn't govern.

the knot · behind the gear

Everything else, one sheet deep.

The reading screen holds only the day. The rest lives in the knot, and the sheet is the same every time you open it. Nothing is promoted, and nothing moves.
