Problem & Intent

Status, owner & next action

Awaiting approval. Triggered by the owner reporting that pressing the weave to seal “doesn't seem intuitive — it doesn't look like a button”. Next action: owner approves or amends; then S01 in a fresh ../thread-open-row worktree. Commit, push, PR and merge each still need explicit authorization.

Outcome and constraints

The seal is the app's daily commitment gesture and the thing E1 exists to measure, so it must stay a 1.2s hold that writes the same events. What changes is what the reader touches, and what the screen shows while they do it. The mark today is a hard-coded 7-chapter, 4-row loom in flat indigo (SealZone.tsx, geometry(168, 96, 7, Array(5).fill(true))) — cloth the reader never wove, contradicting the real bolt rendered 200pt below it, and drawn so that today's row is invisible rather than open.

Constraints: offline, single-user, synthetic data only in artifacts; no new dependency, font or icon set; values from src/ui/tokens.ts; motion honours OS reduce-motion; Reanimated worklets stay on the UI thread; Vitest is node-only, so nothing here can be proven by rendering a component.

Scope and non-goals

In scope: the seal's hold path becomes today's open row in the reader's own bolt with a shuttle as the instrument; the seal and weave zones merge into one zone with two states; ThreadRail is fixed to weave its full height and calmed at its true width.

Non-goals: the gesture's timings and events; the tap / screen-reader / reduced-motion fallback and the two-tap link; anything in src/lab/; the knot's compact weave; the translation-credit move (belongs to the parked knot-translation-switch plan).

Requirements (confirmed)

User stories

  1. As a reader finishing today's chapter, I want the thing I am meant to touch to look like an object I can pick up, so that I do not have to guess that a picture is a control.
  2. As a reader, I want the cloth on the seal screen to be my bolt — my book's chapter count, my days, my book's dye — so that nothing on the screen is decorative filler.
  3. As a reader, I want today drawn as a genuine gap in the cloth, so that the thing I am asked to close is visibly unfinished.
  4. As a reader, I want the warp below today to run on into the days I have not read, so that the row reads as threads under tension rather than tally marks.
  5. As a reader, I want the shuttle to carry the weft across as I hold, so that the gesture and its result are one movement.
  6. As a reader, I want the slack to leave the warp as the pass crosses, so that holding feels like work rather than waiting.
  7. As a reader completing the hold, I want the new row to beat up against the cloth, so that committing has a physical end rather than a state flip.
  8. As a reader who has just sealed, I want one cloth on screen with today woven into it, so that I never see two looms at once.
  9. As a reader, I want the bolt to appear only after I seal, so that the record of days I have read is not shown to me before I have earned today.
  10. As a reader, I want the book name and the day count to arrive with the sealed bolt, so that the summary belongs to the finished state.
  11. As a reader opening the knot, I want the compact weave summary to keep working exactly as it does now, so that merging the flow zones costs me nothing elsewhere.
  12. As a reader scrolling a chapter, I want the rail's cloth to weave the whole way down to its own fell mark, so that the edge does not appear to give up halfway.
  13. As a reader, I want the rail's three threads to stay in their own lanes, so that the left edge reads as a narrow cloth rather than a tangle.
  14. As a reader reaching the end of the reading, I want the rail's fell to arrive at the open row and stop there, so that the line I have watched descend all session ends on the thing I am asked to close.
  15. As a reader, I want the rail to keep tracking scroll exactly as it does today, including under reduced motion, so that a direct-manipulation indicator never lies about position.
  16. As a reader who releases the hold early, I want the pass to withdraw and the warp to go slack again, so that abandoning is as legible as committing.
  17. As a reader on the first day, I want a short bounded invitation on the shuttle — three cycles, then still — so that I learn the movement once without being nagged.
  18. As a reader with reduce-motion enabled, I want the open row and its gap to be fully legible with nothing animating, so that the design does not depend on movement.
  19. As a reader using a screen reader, I want the instrument to carry the accessible name and role while the cloth is treated as decoration, so that there is exactly one thing to activate.
  20. As a reader who has not met the completion floor, I want the disabled state and its helper text to behave exactly as they do today, so that E4's floor still governs when sealing is possible.
  21. As the lab (a silent actor), I want seal and hold_cancel written with the same shape, timing and conditions as before, so that E1's arms and the §11 mechanic-friction rung stay comparable across this change.
  22. As a reader on a very long book, I want the seal screen to stay a constant size, so that day 60 is as easy to seal as day 6.
  23. As a reader on a one-chapter book, I want the open row to still be a sane shape, so that narrow cloth does not produce a broken instrument.

Testing Decisions

  • Seam: pure geometry only. The visual work is React Native components and the suite is node-only with no renderer, so anything that must be proven is extracted into src/ui/rail.ts and src/ui/openRow.ts as exported functions, and the components stay thin.
  • Prior art: test/loom.test.ts for pure geometry assertions (no fakes, no db); test/bolt.test.ts for the derived-data seam if a query is ever needed.
  • Regression to encode: the rail's coverage bug — assert that the shipped ROW_PITCH 20 formula does not cover a 736pt screen and that the new one does, so it cannot silently return.
  • Guard to re-run: test/render-path-cost.test.ts — the seal zone now draws cloth before sealing, so the per-render path budget must be re-checked.
  • Deliberately not unit-tested: the shuttle's travel animation, haptics, press states, and anything needing a rendered tree or a gesture handler. No renderer exists, and faking one would prove nothing; owner-verified on a dev build instead.
  • Owner spot-check (optional, dev build): the hold still seals and still cancels on early release; the rail reaches the shuttle at the end of a chapter; the knot's compact weave still renders.

Direction questions

Intent — a loom mid-work: the warp, the fell, and the shuttle in your hand. An extension of the existing loom language, not a new pattern; the one genuinely new idea is that the instrument and the record are different objects.

Hygiene — dyeFor(book) for cloth, weft and shuttle; color.warp for bare warp; ink40 for the rubric; font.mono for it; seal.* for timings; control.minTarget 44 via hitSlop. No new value.

Details — pressed (shuttle scales .94 in 140ms plus a touch-down haptic); progress (shuttle travels, weft follows, slack leaves the warp); success (beat-up, then the bolt); cancel (withdraw over 220ms, warp goes slack again); disabled (canSeal false — existing helper text, unchanged); sealed (one bolt, label and caption); reduced motion (static row plus the existing fallback button); screen reader (fallback button, cloth silent); large font scale (the rubric wraps; the cloth is measured, not fixed).

Execution

Grade C3: 10 files across src/ui and src/flow, three slices with disjoint ownership, and a shipped component (ThreadRail) changed independently of the feature that motivated it. Not C4 — this touches none of the protected mechanisms (no event-log migration, no PRNG, no notification scheduling, no backup crypto, no partner hand-off, no destructive reset) and writes no new event.

The authoritative file recipes, slice dependencies, commands, budgets, branching strategy, delegation decisions and rollback steps are in exec.md. Live status is recorded only in PROGRESS.md.

S01 — the rail weaves its full height and stops tangling; independently shippable, and worth shipping whatever happens to the rest. Serial waves, inline execution: the slices are small enough that delegation would cost more than it saves, and delegation is not authorized.
