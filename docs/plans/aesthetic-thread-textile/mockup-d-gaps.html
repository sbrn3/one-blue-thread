direction c · the loom — missed days

A missed day isn't an empty box. It's an open place in the cloth.

The cloth grows with the calendar, not with your effort — so a day you didn't read still takes up length. It just has no weft in it. You can see straight through. The warp across a gap has nothing holding it at its proper spacing, so it goes slack and drifts, exactly as it would on a real loom. Weavers have a name for the line this leaves when you stop and come back: a set mark. It never comes out.

the point

The texture of the cloth is the texture of the practice.

Same book, same dye, same number of days on the calendar — three different readers. Nothing is scored, nothing is red, nothing says "you failed." The cloth just tells the truth, and open cloth is still cloth.

what this replaces

Not a calendar with holes in it.

Today the weave zone is a 15-column grid of filled and dashed squares — the same contribution-graph everyone has seen. The difference is that a grid says you missed 6 of 30, and cloth says this is what your month felt like. One is a score. The other is a record.
