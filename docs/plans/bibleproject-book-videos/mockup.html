Book bookends & takeaways

This replaces the earlier mockup rounds A–E, which are archived in mockup-rounds-1-2.html. The page has five parts: what those rounds taught us, the user stories, the method, three candidates with the chosen direction, and the questions left for you.

2 · User stories

  1. Overview video
  2. As a reader starting a book, I see BibleProject's overview link on my first sitting, so I get the book's shape before I read.
  3. As a reader of a two-part book, I see both parts, labelled, so I can watch the one for where I am.
  4. As a reader who has just finished, I see the overview again, so I can watch it with the whole book fresh.
  5. As a reader, I can open any book's overview later from Reading history, so skipping it on day 1 loses nothing.
  6. As a reader whose link fails to open, I see the address to type, and nothing else breaks.
  7. As a reader of a book that shares a video (1–3 John, Ezra–Nehemiah…), I get that video, honestly labelled.
  8. Writing a line
  9. As a reader who has just sealed, I'm offered one line to keep, never required to write one, so consolidating is a choice, not a chore.
  10. As a reader writing, I see today's verses again and can tap the ones my line is about, so re-reading becomes the consolidating. If I pick nothing, the line belongs to the whole passage.
  11. As a reader, the field stays above the keyboard, holds 280 characters with a visible count, and can't be saved blank.
  12. As a reader who closes without saving, nothing is kept and nothing is logged.
  13. As a reader, I keep one line per day. If I've already written today's, Dismissal shows it and lets me edit it.
  14. As a reader who reopens later the same day, my line still belongs to the passage I sealed, not tomorrow's chapter.
  15. As a reader with the app open past 4 AM, my line belongs to the day I sealed.
  16. Reading it back
  17. As a reader finishing a book, I see this reading's lines oldest first, each under the verse it's about, so the book comes back to me in Scripture and in my words. Today's line appears as soon as I save it.
  18. As a reader who wrote no lines, "You finished" shows no empty list and no nudge.
  19. As a reader, I can open any book in Reading history, finished or in progress, and see the same read-back and its overview.
  20. As a reader re-reading a book, its history shows every reading's lines, dated. "You finished" shows only this reading's.
  21. As a reader, tapping a reference opens that chapter with those verses highlighted.
  22. As a reader, I can edit a past line's words or verses, or delete it after confirming.
  23. As a reader whose licensed translation fails to load, I still see my lines and their references, never a blank screen.
  24. Data, privacy, access
  25. As a reader, my lines are in my encrypted backup and come back on restore. Restoring an older backup without lines warns that they'll be replaced.
  26. As a reader who unravels the account, my lines are erased with everything else.
  27. As the lab (a silent actor), I never see lines. Nothing is logged and no experiment reads them.
  28. As any reader: 44pt targets; labelled controls; the read-back reads verse, then line, then reference; works at the largest font scale; honours reduced motion.

3 · How we avoid a vibe-coded design

Design the moment, not the widgetEach surface gets a one-sentence job taken from a story. Anything that doesn't serve that job is cut before styling starts.
Take the rules from the product's own constitutionScripture-first voice: verse first, verbatim and attributed, then your words in a different voice. The fell line is the only progress indicator, so no counts. One reader, so no feed.
Meaning before metaphorA visual device must be legible without being explained. If it needs a caption, it goes.
Type and space firstCompose with the three existing faces and the spacing scale. Add chrome (borders, panels, pills) only when a story needs an affordance.
Ground it in the real appNext round, sketch over screenshots from the dev build on your phone instead of HTML approximations of it.
Critique before showingCould another app ship this unchanged? Does any element exist only to look designed? Does it break a voice or §13.6 rule? Any yes sends it back.

4 · Round 3: three candidates, one chosen

The diagnosis: every earlier option treated a takeaway as a note kept beside the Bible, and any notes app ships that. Printed Bibles already have a place for exactly this. The Geneva Bible opens each book with The Argument, the 1611 King James puts a one-line headnote before every chapter, and both have a contents page. The BibleProject video is the Argument, your daily line is the headnote, and the end-of-book summary is the book's contents page, in your words. The full divergence log is in git history, in the deleted level-up.md (git history).

Recommended

1 · Headnotes

Your lines become the book's apparatus: a contents page with chapter numbers, dot leaders and references. A chapter you didn't write about shows just its number, the bare-warp principle. Distinctive, legible without explanation, and its peak is finishing the book.
Not chosen

2 · Pairs (F)

A verse, then your line. Honest about voice, but a column of quote-plus-note is Kindle highlights. Another app ships it unchanged. Its "choose a verse" move survives as the optional verse range.
Borrowed, later slice

3 · Relay

Yesterday's line echoes under the cue at Arrival: spaced recall from your own words. Good mechanism but a small surface, so it becomes an optional follow-on, not the direction.

5 · "Headnotes", clean, with a little art

This is round 3's layout and behaviour, unchanged: dotted contents, your lines in the app's own voice, nothing encoded in the art. Round 4's woven cloth inside the rows was tacky, so it's gone. The art is now ornament only: a single fine thread, the way printed books use a headpiece to open a section and a tailpiece to close it. It's one hairline in the thread colour, at two moments: where a book begins and where it ends. It means nothing, and it doesn't have to.
