# Aesthetic families and DNA

Adapted from ryanthedev/design-for-ai (`archetypes.md`, `design-dna.md`) and its seeded `dealer` idea. Convergence is broken at construction level, not by prompting harder.

## Two-stage funnel

1. **Archetype** - the product's emotional relationship with its audience (not the founder's self-image). Derive 2 to 3 candidates from purpose + audience + personality words. The twelve: Innocent, Everyman, Hero, Outlaw, Explorer, Creator, Ruler, Magician, Lover, Caregiver, Jester, Sage.
   For One Blue Thread the honest set is roughly **Sage** (scripture, quiet authority, no gimmicks), **Everyman** (a personal tool for one reader, not a product), **Innocent** (calm, unburdened, nothing to defend — no streaks).
2. **Aesthetic family** - a concrete, constrained visual system the archetype maps to.

## Content has a vote

Dense data and tables pull toward Swiss / Terminal / instrument families. Long-form pulls toward Editorial — One Blue Thread's scripture surface lives here (a reading, not a screen). A reading-habit tool also has a records-and-notation vote (almanac, devotional, ship's log). Let the content type override a family the archetype alone would suggest.

## Register rule

Product surfaces (the daily working views) use the *restrained* end of a family. Brand surfaces (a landing page, an about screen) can commit fully. Do not bring full brand expression into a form the user fills in every shift.

## Families to draw from

Editorial minimalism, Swiss modernism, Terminal-core / instrument, Neo-brutalist, Field notebook / documentary, Ledger / bookkeeping, Art deco, Soft futurism, Retro-futurist, Nature-distilled (muted earthy). Each varies on three dials: typographic personality (humanist serif -> grotesque -> mono), color commitment (muted -> neon), motion character (near-still -> fluid).

## Fix three axes per candidate (the "deal")

Take these out of vibe and make them explicit:

- **Aesthetic family** - name one. Optionally borrow *one* axis (type, or color, or composition) from a second family. That cross is the combination that does not exist as a cluster in training data. No single reference anchors two candidates.
- **Layout discipline** - strict baseline grid / asymmetric column / single dominant object / dense table-first. Spread this across candidates; at least one candidate should invert an assumption another holds.
- **Signature move** - the one gesture a viewer would describe back afterwards.

## Pins

Anything the user has stated about taste locks that axis. It is a constraint, not a preference to weigh against the statistically familiar option. The skill fills the unpinned axes.

## Critique before choice

Do not pick by ambient reaction - that is how the most familiar candidate wins by default. Score each candidate on:

- **Distinctiveness** - recognisable without the logo, or clustered near the center of the distribution?
- **Register fit** - do the moments of visual emphasis land on genuine emotional peaks in the user's task, or are they decoration?
- **Tells scan** - does the spec reproduce anything in `ai-tells.md`?

## Palette

Build color as a ramp with contrast solved by construction (OKLCH lightness steps, WCAG 4.5:1 for text checked up front), then bind every step to an existing token. Never hand-pick hex per element and adjust contrast later.
